Forecast methodology

The Score gives equal weight to two improvements over a similar-day forecast. One rewards lower price error. The other rewards capturing battery revenue the baseline would have missed.

Score = price error reduction %+battery revenue shortfall recovered % 2

Scoring methodology

We compare each model with the naïve forecast on the same market-days. We pool price errors and battery trading margins across the selected markets and months before calculating the Score.

Naïve forecast

The similar-day baseline copies a previously cleared price for each quarter-hour. For delivery on Tuesday through Friday, it uses the previous day (D−1). For Saturday through Monday, it uses the same day a week earlier (D−7). It matches prices by local clock time, accounting for clock changes.

Price error

Mean absolute error (MAE) is the average absolute difference between the model’s median price forecast and the cleared price, in €/MWh. Error reduction is 100 × (1 − model MAE ÷ naïve MAE).

Revenue capture and the two-hour battery

We schedule a price-taking 1 MW / 2 MWh battery on each model’s median forecast and settle that schedule at the cleared prices. The battery has 90% round-trip efficiency, starts and ends each day half full and can discharge up to one equivalent full cycle per day.

Capture is 100 × realised trading margin ÷ margin with perfect knowledge of prices, using identical constraints. If naïve captures 60% and a model captures 80%, the leaderboard shows 80% capture. The Score credits the model with recovering half the remaining opportunity, (80 − 60) ÷ (100 − 60) = 50%.

The simulation covers day-ahead arbitrage only. It excludes intraday and balancing trades, fees, imbalance costs and battery degradation.

How models are ranked

For a multi-month rank, a model must complete every contributing month with the same checkpoint. We rank unrounded scores from highest to lowest. Exact ties share a rank.

Data source

ENTSO-E supplies electricity data for all eight bidding zones. The European Energy Exchange (EEX) publishes the gas and carbon prices. For gaps in the gas series, we use the same index from Gasunie Transport Services.

Electricity prices and forecasts

We use cleared day-ahead prices (A44), day-ahead demand forecasts (A65), and wind and solar generation forecasts (A69). The D−2 auction sets prices for every quarter-hour of D−1, through midnight. The D−1 auction sets the prices for D that we use for scoring. We match each price to its delivery quarter in the bidding zone’s local time. ENTSO-E Transparency Platform ↗

ENTSO-E requires the first day-ahead demand forecast at least two hours before the auction closes and updates when demand changes significantly. Its day-ahead wind and solar series retains the latest forecast available at 18:00 on D−1. The archived versions therefore cannot establish exactly what a forecaster saw at noon. Gas and carbon prices

We use the final Dutch TTF neutral gas price for gas day D−2. EEX publishes it shortly after that gas day ends at 06:00 on D−1. Gasunie Transport Services supplies the same index for dates missing from the EEX archive. EEX gas index ↗ · Gasunie index definition ↗

For carbon, we use the price from the latest successful EU emissions allowance auction. An auction on the electricity bidding day qualifies only if a record saved by noon already contains its result. Otherwise, we use the previous auction price. EEX carbon auctions ↗

How missing data affects coverage

If a bidding zone lacks a required historical price or a demand, wind or solar forecast for any delivery quarter, we skip that day for every model. Gaps in historical demand, wind and solar forecasts stay marked as missing. A missing delivery price affects scoring only.
